The story so far

Coming into Trendsoft, most of what I'd built before was against someone else's spec — a clear brief, a defined scope, follow the lines. Here I was handed something different: independently lead a full UI/UX redesign of the company's main consultancy website, under supervisor review rather than under a strict brief. That's a small-sounding shift, but it changed how I thought about my own work. Every layout choice, every interaction pattern, was now a decision I had to be able to defend, not a checkbox someone else had already ticked.

The redesign itself was a grind in the best way. Turning a legacy interface into something modern and responsive meant writing PHP, JavaScript, and CSS modules that had to look and behave the same regardless of what browser someone happened to open the site in — you can't assume everyone's on the same setup. Cross-browser quirks I'd mostly just read about before became my problem to actually solve, one inconsistency at a time.

Alongside the redesign, I got pulled into some of the company's .NET and Blazor work, helping build and maintain scalable components on an entirely different stack. Jumping between the PHP-driven front end I owned outright and a C#/Blazor codebase I was still learning meant holding two different mental models at once — which turned out to be its own kind of useful skill.

The part that's stuck with me most, though, is the testing and debugging discipline that comes with owning something end-to-end. When you're the one who led the redesign, "it works on my machine" isn't good enough — there's real time spent chasing down edge cases and iterating until the thing is stable enough for people who actually use the site to depend on it. That's a different kind of pressure than any solo side project gives you.

Challenges & learnings

Challenges

  • Rebuilding a legacy interface without breaking functionality people already relied on.
  • Cross-browser inconsistencies in layout, spacing, and font rendering.
  • Balancing redesign work with .NET/Blazor contributions on a completely different stack.

Learnings

  • Owning a project end-to-end means every decision needs a reason, not just a preference.
  • Testing across browsers early saves far more time than fixing it all at the end.
  • Working across PHP and Blazor at once sharpened how quickly I can context-switch between stacks.
