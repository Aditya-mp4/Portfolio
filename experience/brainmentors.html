The story

Brain Mentors was my first internship, full stop — the first time anyone handed me something real and said "build this, and other people are going to use it." That one sentence made the stakes feel different from any class project I'd done up to that point.

The task was a quiz application in Flutter, and I built it end-to-end — the UI, the state management, the logic that actually decided whether an answer was right and kept score across questions. I remember getting noticeably more careful about edge cases once it hit me that this wasn't going into a folder to be graded once and forgotten — it was going to ship.

Being new to working on an actual team, I leaned a lot on testing and refinement alongside the people around me before anything went out the door — catching the small stuff, like a state that didn't reset properly or a score that didn't update the way it should, that's obvious in hindsight but easy to miss when you're building something like this for the first time.

Looking back, Brain Mentors is where I first felt that specific mix of nerves and pride that comes from shipping something real alongside a team, and it's a feeling I kept chasing in every internship since.

Challenges & learnings

😤 Challenges

  • Keeping quiz state — score, progress, current question — consistent across screens.
  • Being new to real team workflows and figuring out how to ask for and give feedback.
  • Catching edge cases that only showed up once other people started testing it.

💡 Learnings

  • Building for someone else changes how carefully you think through edge cases.
  • Testing and refinement with a team catches things solo testing misses.
  • This first internship shaped what I looked for in every internship after it.
