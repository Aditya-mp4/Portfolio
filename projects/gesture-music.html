Music System

Wave your hand. Make music. A real-time computer vision system that reads your fingers through a webcam and turns them into MIDI-style notes — no keyboard, no controller, just a hand in the air.

Play it yourself

Wave at your camera 👋

This runs entirely in your browser using your webcam — nothing is recorded, saved, or uploaded anywhere. Hit start, allow camera access, and move your hand in front of the camera to play notes.

idle

How it works: number of fingers raised picks a note on a pentatonic scale, and how high your hand sits controls how loud it plays — the same core idea as the real MIDI project, rebuilt with the Web Audio API so it can run right here on this page. Needs a webcam and a recent Chrome, Edge, or Firefox. If the demo can't start (no camera, blocked permission, older browser), scroll down for a walkthrough of exactly how the full system works instead.

What it does

👋

Real-time hand tracking

MediaPipe detects 21 hand landmarks per frame with sub-20ms latency, entirely on-device.

🎵

Gesture → MIDI mapping

Custom logic maps finger combinations and hand position to notes and control signals.

🎛️

DAW integration

MIDI output feeds directly into Ableton, FL Studio, or GarageBand — no plugins required.

🔄

Iteratively refined

Tuned across multiple test sessions to cut jitter, false triggers, and control drift.

Why I built this

I've always split my time between two things that don't usually share a room: writing code and messing around with music. This project is where I finally got them to meet. The idea was almost embarrassingly simple — what if my hand could just be the instrument? No MIDI keyboard, no pads, just a webcam and a gesture.

The first working version was rough. Hand tremors that any human doesn't even notice were enough to fire a dozen phantom note-on events a second — my "instrument" sounded like it had stage fright. I spent a lot of late nights adding debounce buffers and confidence thresholds until the jitter finally calmed down, and threading the OpenCV capture separately from the MIDI output cut a good chunk of latency once the pipeline stopped fighting itself for CPU time.

What surprised me most was how much of this became a design problem instead of a machine-learning one. MediaPipe is very good at telling you where a hand is — it says nothing about whether the gesture you just made was musically useful. I ended up running informal test sessions with anyone who'd let me point a camera at their hand, watching how naturally (or unnaturally) they landed on the gesture vocabulary I'd picked, and adjusting from there.

The live demo above is a browser-native rebuild of the same core idea, so you don't have to take my word for any of this — go wave at your camera and hear it for yourself.

# Gesture detection core logic
def get_fingers_up(hand_landmarks):
  fingers = []
  tips = [8, 12, 16, 20] # index, middle, ring, pinky
  for tip in tips:
    fingers.append(hand_landmarks[tip].y < hand_landmarks[tip - 2].y)
  return fingers

def map_to_midi(fingers, hand_y):
  note = SCALE[fingers.count(True)] # fingers up → note
  velocity = int(127 * (1 - hand_y)) # y position → velocity
  midi_out.note_on(note, velocity)

Challenges & learnings

😤 Challenges

  • Gesture jitter — tiny hand tremors caused constant false MIDI triggers.
  • Lighting variance made MediaPipe's confidence drop in low or harsh light.
  • MIDI latency spiked whenever the CPU was under load from OpenCV processing.
  • Designing a gesture vocabulary that felt intuitive to actually play.

💡 Learnings

  • Debounce buffers and confidence thresholds dramatically reduce jitter.
  • Threading capture separately from MIDI output cut latency by roughly 40%.
  • Human-centered design matters — people need to learn a system like this in minutes, not hours.
  • Iterative test sessions are the only real way to validate gesture UX.
