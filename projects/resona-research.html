Resona

A hand in the air becomes an instrument.

Aditya Kumar Mehra · Yash Rajvansh · Saakshi Verma

01 / the question

Can a hand in the air become a musical instrument?

No keys. No controller. No touch — only motion.

02 / why it matters

Making music is locked behind hardware.

MIDI keyboards, control surfaces, years of practice — a wall between people and sound.

For beginners, and for anyone with limited mobility, that wall stands higher still.

03 / what came before

Touchless music already existed — but never freely.

The Theremin and data gloves — antennas and wearables.

Leap Motion, Myo, Kinect — proprietary, bulky, costly.

Plain webcam vision — undone by light and clutter.

04 / the gap

MediaPipe can see a hand — twenty-one points, in real time, on any laptop.

But it doesn't know what the hand is saying.

Deep learning could — at the cost of GPUs, datasets, and training.

The gap — read the gesture live. No training. No special hardware.

05 / the proposal

So we built Resona.

A webcam reads your hand. Rules read the gesture. MIDI turns it into sound.

No wearables. No GPU. No training.

06 / how it works

One — the webcam watches, thirty frames a second.

Two — MediaPipe maps twenty-one points across the hand.

Three — geometry reads the pose: bends, distances, a pinch.

Four — rules name the gesture. Eight of them.

08 / the vocabulary

Eight gestures. A handful of rules.

Open palmplay · pause
Pinchtrigger a note
Swipe up · downvolume
Move left · rightpitch

A pinch is just two fingertips closer than thirty pixels.

Built in Python — OpenCV, MediaPipe, and a thin line of MIDI.

10 / what's next

Let it learn your own gestures.

Let it hear your mood.

Two hands. Many players.

Depth cameras. AR. The stage.

11 / the idea

A standard webcam turned motion into music.

Expression, unlocked — for anyone with a hand to raise.

A foundation for human–computer interaction, interactive art, and assistive tech.
