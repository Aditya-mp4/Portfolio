Why I built this

Most of what I build starts from curiosity, but this one started from something closer to obligation. Accessible tech is one of the few areas where a computer vision project isn't just a cool demo — it can genuinely remove a barrier for someone. That pull toward assistive technology, toward building something that actually helps someone communicate, is what got me into this project in the first place.

The gnarliest technical problem was accuracy on visually similar signs. A lot of sign language gestures differ by something as small as finger spacing or a slight wrist rotation, and early on the classifier would confidently get these confused with each other. Getting that right meant paying much closer attention to hand segmentation and landmark precision than I initially expected — this wasn't a project where "mostly right" was good enough, because a wrong word spoken out loud is worse than no word at all.

That's also what taught me how much UX simplicity matters here, more than in almost anything else I've built. If someone is relying on this to communicate, every extra tap, every confusing screen, every moment of "wait, did it hear me?" is a real cost, not just a minor annoyance. I ended up stripping the Flutter interface down again and again until it was basically just a camera view and a transcript — nothing to think about, nothing standing between a gesture and a spoken word.

Challenges & learnings

😤 Challenges

  • Visually similar signs were easy for the classifier to confuse with each other.
  • Latency across the Python → Flask → Flutter pipeline was initially too high for natural use.
  • Cluttered backgrounds made hand segmentation less reliable outside controlled lighting.
  • Designing a UX simple enough to not get in the way of the person actually using it.

💡 Learnings

  • Tighter hand segmentation and landmark precision cut down misclassification significantly.
  • Caching frequent sign-to-text mappings on the client trimmed round-trip latency.
  • Assistive tech needs ruthlessly simple UX — every extra tap is a barrier, not a feature.
  • Cross-platform development with Flutter saved real time versus building native apps twice.
